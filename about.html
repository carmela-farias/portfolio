The path looks strange on paper

I studied industrial design. Then I tested software. Then I designed it. Now I build it.

Written out like that it reads like someone who couldn't make up her mind. It's the opposite. It's the same instinct turning up four times.

Industrial design taught me that a drawing isn't a product until someone can manufacture it. QA taught me that a product isn't finished until it survives contact with real users. Front-end taught me that a design isn't a decision until it exists in the codebase.

Every move has been toward the place where the work stops being a proposal and starts being a thing.

What that looks like now

At Orca Scan I own UX end to end and the front-end layer underneath it — flows, MVP scoping, visual design, the design system, and the CSS that puts all of it in production.

The design system is the part I'm most attached to. I've maintained it since 2023, not as a Figma library but as code: tokens for colour, type, spacing, radii, shadows, icons and components, versioned alongside the product. Designers hand over systems all the time. Keeping one alive in a codebase that ships every week is a different job.

Before that I led QA at Singular Agency, which is how I ended up designing there — I kept finding UX problems underneath the bugs I was logging, and a client asked for me by name. I also taught Design Thinking to 130+ people at Digital House, which is still the fastest way I know to find out whether you actually understand something.

How I work

I'd rather ship something small that's right than present something large that isn't. Scope is a design decision and most of the ones worth making are subtractions.

I write the code when writing the code is what's needed. CSS is mine end to end. For JavaScript logic I work with AI, which is how I've shipped things I couldn't have written alone two years ago. I'd rather say that plainly than overstate what I do without help.

I'm the person who closes the gap. Between design and development, between what was decided and what got built, between the file and the thing people actually use. That's been the job in every role I've had, whatever the title said.
